Flow teardown Headspace · Calm · Mindspace August 2026

We asked five questions and used one of the answers

Fourteen flows compared end to end. Mindspace matched or beat both rivals on most of them — but the onboarding quiz, the single most studied screen in this category, wrote four of its five answers to a database column that nothing ever read. Six of the eight fixes below have since shipped; what is left needs a decision rather than a build.

The finding

Verified in the source, not inferred

Critical — dead personalisation — now fixed

The quiz collected intent and threw it away

apps/mobile/src/app/(onboarding)/quiz.tsx asks five questions across five screens with a progress bar. The answers post to PUT /users/me/onboarding, land in onboarding_profiles, and are read back onto the user object. Only one of them changes anything the user ever sees.

Question Stored as What consumes it
What brings you here? goals USED — pickDaily() weights the daily pick
Have you meditated before? experience_level NOTHING
How are you sleeping? sleep_quality NOTHING
How much time can you give it? daily_minutes NOTHING
When suits you best? preferred_time NOTHING

The last row is the expensive one. We ask when someone intends to practise, store it, and then schedule their reminder at reminder_time DEFAULT '08:00' — the column default, for everyone. A user who taps Evening gets an eight in the morning notification. Headspace measured exactly this mechanic: getting users to commit to a specific time drove a 7.5% lift in app opens and 4% more unique return days during trial. We do the asking and skip the acting.

Someone who answered "Never tried it" and "3 minutes" was offered the same library, at the same lengths, as a decade-long practitioner with twenty minutes. All five answers now change what the app does, and the quiz ends on a recap that reads the plan back.

Uncovered while fixing it

The free catalogue cannot honour the length answer

Wiring daily_minutes exposed a content problem underneath it. The catalogue holds ten free meditations — every one of them in beginners, every one exactly ten minutes. Everything else is Pro.

So a free user who says "3 minutes" gets a ten-minute session, and one who says "20 minutes" gets the same ten-minute session. The preference works; there is nothing for it to choose between. The fallback picks the closest available length and the reason line stops claiming a fit it did not achieve, but no amount of code fixes a catalogue with one duration in it. This is a content decision, and it currently undercuts the personalisation on the tier most users experience first.

Flow by flow

Fourteen flows, first tap to last

Each block is the same journey in all three apps. The verdict is against the pair, not against either one — parity means we are level with the better of the two.

F01

First run — install to first tap

Parity
Headspace

Welcome screens that set expectations with narrative framing before anything is asked. Onboarding is deliberately paced to preview what the product is for.

Calm

Ambient scene and sound on launch. Sign-up is mandatory before the paywall, but the user feels the product first.

Mindspace

welcome.tsx → sign-in.tsx. Gradient hero, Apple/Google/email, and a guest path. Ambient audio loop plays on home open.

Verdict Level. The guest path is arguably better than Calm's mandatory sign-up — one tap to a working app.
F02

Personalisation quiz

Fixed
Headspace
  1. Experience level
  2. Session length
  3. What brings you here
  4. When you'll meditate
  5. Recap of your answers
Calm

Goals and experience asked before any premium prompt. Declared data personalises the home screen immediately, which is how they beat the cold-start problem.

Mindspace

Five questions with a progress bar and per-step gradient backdrops — the best-looking of the three — now closing on a recap that reads the plan back.

Verdict Was the worst flow in the product: four of five answers were stored and never read. All five now do something.
Shipped preferred_time writes through to the reminder — evening becomes 20:00, afternoon 13:00 — but only over the untouched 08:00 default, so a time set in Settings is never overwritten. daily_minutes constrains the daily pick by duration and orders by closeness; experience_level folds beginner content into a new user's pool; sleep_quality: poor surfaces the sleep row all day. The quiz now ends on a recap of the plan.
F03

First session

Ahead
Headspace

Guided into a 3–5 minute meditation straight out of the questions, then asked how it felt.

Calm

Lands on a personalised home with a tooltip pointing at where to begin. Self-paced rather than pushed.

Mindspace

The quiz ends at /breathe?onboarding=1 — a real breathing exercise with haptic phase cues, not a form. Practice is the first experience.

Verdict We take Headspace's strongest move and arguably improve on it: breathing needs no audio download and works before any entitlement check.
F04

Paywall and trial

Fixed
Headspace

Paywall inside the onboarding sequence, before the habit exists. Trial framing throughout.

Calm

Paywall presented during onboarding — most purchases happen here. Seven-day trial on the annual plan, strikethrough pricing.

Mindspace

Now offered once at the end of onboarding, after the first breathing exercise. The six reactive entry points remain for anyone who meets a lock later.

Verdict Was a punishment screen every user met at a moment of friction. It now also appears as an offer, at the only honest moment to ask — once they have felt what the app does.
Shipped /paywall?offer=1 changes the framing: different headline and copy, and a visible Keep using the free plan button. A lone ✕ in the corner reads as a trap at the end of onboarding, and someone who feels cornered on day one is not the one who renews on day thirty.
F05

Navigation and information architecture

Gap
Headspace

Three tabs: Today, Explore, Profile. They previously had meditate / focus / movement / sleep tabs and removed them after user research.

Calm

Home-led, with Sleep given its own prominent destination — the category they own commercially.

Mindspace

Five tabs: Today, Explore, Courses, Stats, Profile.

Verdict Headspace ran this experiment and simplified. Courses and Stats are both reachable from Today and Profile; as tabs they spend permanent screen real estate on destinations most users visit rarely. Worth testing three.
F06

The daily return loop

Gap
Headspace

Today tab offers one-tap activities segmented by length and by time of day — morning, afternoon, night.

Calm

Daily Calm: a brand-new original ~10 minute session every single day. Content freshness is a pull reason that works independently of the streak.

Mindspace

A daily recommendation chosen from a static library by goals, plus an evening wind-down row after 20:00 local. Nothing is new on any given day.

Verdict The distinction matters. A recommendation can repeat and users notice; a dated original cannot. This is the value-erosion problem from the retention review, and it is a content-operations commitment rather than a code change.
F07

Session entry — taps to audio

Mixed
Headspace

One tap from Today into playback. The tab exists to remove the choosing step.

Calm

One tap from the home hero; browse paths go through a detail screen.

Mindspace

Home hero: one tap — plays and pushes the player directly. Explore: two taps — card → detail screen → "Play session". Reminder and widget now go straight to /start.

Verdict The primary path is level. The browse path costs an extra tap, which is defensible — a detail screen carries duration, teacher and download — but worth a play affordance directly on the card.
F08

Player controls

Fixed
Headspace

Deliberately minimal, advanced settings tucked away. Player art conveys the mood of the session.

Calm

Standard transport plus playback speed and one-tap share. Background art matches the session's tone.

Mindspace

Play/pause, rewind 10s, background-sound mixer, volume, minimise, end session, playback speed and a sleep timer. Category-gradient artwork. No forward skip or share.

Verdict The mixer remains an advantage neither rival matches, and the two real omissions are closed.
Shipped Speed cycles 1× → 1.25× → 1.5× → 0.75× with pitch correction, sitting below the transport so the three primary controls stay unambiguous. The sleep timer (5–45 min) appears only on sleepcast-style formats, and ends the session rather than pausing — someone who fell asleep to a sleepcast practised, and losing their day to a timer they set themselves would be perverse.
F09

Post-session

Ahead
Headspace

Asks how you feel after finishing.

Calm

Check-in prompt; mood feeds recommendations.

Mindspace

Animated summary: streak, this-session / this-week / all-time minutes, newly unlocked badges, course completion, share sheet, and a post-session mood prompt that carries context=post_session. Now also reports a protected streak.

Verdict Clearly the richest of the three, and the only one that closes the pre/post mood loop explicitly.
F10

Check-ins

Fixed
Headspace

Daily mood prompt on open, plus a periodic longer questionnaire covering stress, overwhelm and anger — closer to journalling.

Calm

Check-Ins cover mood, sleep quality, gratitude and reflections — four dimensions.

Mindspace

Mood and sleep — each a 1–5 value plus an encrypted free-text note. Mood carries pre- and post-session contexts; sleep is one reading per night, corrected in place.

Verdict Both dimensions are implemented better than either rival's — encrypted at rest, contextual, trend-charted. Gratitude and reflection remain Calm's, and are a smaller loss than sleep was.
Shipped PUT/GET /activity/sleep, keyed on one row per night so a resubmission corrects rather than duplicates. Sleep was previously asked about once in onboarding and never measured again, so neither the user nor we could tell whether the sleepcasts were doing anything. Notes are encrypted on the same path as mood — verified as ciphertext on disk, not just round-tripping.
F11

Reminders and habit mechanics

Fixed
Headspace

Mindful Moments notifications; a bedtime nudge to begin winding down; onboarding commits the user to a specific practice time.

Calm

Five separate reminder channels, each anchored to a different time of day and habit type — several distinct return touchpoints per day.

Mindspace

Two channels. A practice reminder set from the quiz answer, and a separate bedtime wind-down with its own switch, time and Android channel. Local notifications, so they work offline.

Verdict Two of Calm's five, and the two that matter most — morning practice and bedtime are genuinely different habits. Tapping either now starts audio rather than opening a home screen.
Shipped Bedtime is off by default at 22:00, on its own low-importance channel so it can be silenced without losing the practice reminder. Because rebuilding the schedule cancels everything first, syncAllReminders rewrites both together — otherwise changing one would silently drop the other.
F12

Sleep and bedtime

Mostly fixed
Headspace

Bedtime alarm. Sleepcasts open with a Wind Down — a three-minute breathing exercise — before the narrated landscape.

Calm

Sleep is a headline destination. Sleep Stories, music, soundscapes and wind-down routines, plus a standalone Calm Sleep app.

Mindspace

Bedtime nudge, wind-down chaining, a sleep timer, and nightly sleep tracking. The evening feed row now also appears all day for anyone who said they sleep badly. Still no dedicated sleep destination.

Verdict The ritual now exists over content we already had. What remains is a navigational question — whether sleep deserves its own destination — which folds into the tab decision below.
Shipped Tapping the bedtime nudge runs Headspace's Wind Down shape: a breathing exercise first, then /start?mode=sleep, which picks from the wind-down row rather than the daily recommendation — a focus meditation is the wrong thing to hand someone trying to fall asleep.
F13

Progress and stats

Ahead
Headspace

Profile tab: minutes, sessions, run of days.

Calm

Profile shows streak, mindful minutes and history. A broken streak is repaired by manually editing your history through a support article.

Mindspace

Dedicated dashboard: streak hero, 365-day heatmap, weekly bars, mood trends over 7 and 30 days, achievement badges, and rest-day balance. Streaks are timezone-aware.

Verdict Comfortably ahead, and the automatic rest day is a better answer than Calm's manual history editing — theirs requires knowing a support page exists.
F14

Offline and downloads

Ahead
Headspace

Downloads on paid plans.

Calm

Downloads on paid plans.

Mindspace

Filesystem is the source of truth with a local JSON index, so the manager works in aeroplane mode. Three downloads on free, fifty on Pro.

Verdict Ahead on both mechanism and generosity — neither rival lets a free user take anything offline.

Where we are genuinely ahead

Worth defending, and worth marketing

Timezone-aware streaks

Days are grouped by the user's IANA zone, not server UTC. Travel and late-night sitting do not cost a streak.

Automatic streak insurance

One rest day a month, spent without being asked for. Calm's equivalent is a manual history edit documented in a help centre article.

Encrypted mood and journal notes

Free text is AES-256-GCM encrypted before it reaches Postgres. Neither rival makes this claim, and it is currently unmarketed.

Crisis handling that bypasses the model

Classification runs before the LLM and returns verified helplines directly. Never rate limited, never gated. Ebb escalates through the model.

Free unguided timer and breathing

Both free on every tier, and the timer flows through the same completion, streak and achievement code as guided content.

Background sound mixer inside the player

Per-session ambient layering with independent volume. Neither rival offers this at playback time.

What to fix, in order

Ranked by leverage per unit of build

The first six are built and verified against the running stack. The last two are deliberately not — one needs an experiment, the other a content commitment, and neither should be drifted into by an engineer at midnight.

Turn the quiz answer into the reminder time

Morning / afternoon / evening now map onto 08:00 / 13:00 / 20:00 and write to reminder_time when onboarding completes — but only over the untouched default, so a time chosen in Settings survives re-running the quiz. This is the mechanic Headspace measured at +7.5% app opens.

Shipped

Wire the other three answers

daily_minutes constrains the daily pick by duration and orders by closeness. experience_level folds beginner content into a new user's pool. sleep_quality promotes the sleep row all day for a poor sleeper. Fixing this also surfaced that the free catalogue has only one duration to choose from.

Shipped

Add a recap step and put the offer in onboarding

The quiz now ends on a recap of the plan, and Pro is offered after the first breathing exercise rather than waiting for a collision with a lock. Declining is a visible button, not a corner ✕.

Shipped

Sleep timer and playback speed in the player

The timer (5–45 min) shows only on sleepcast-style formats and ends the session rather than pausing, so the listening still counts. Speed cycles with pitch correction.

Shipped

A bedtime channel, then more channels

A second reminder anchored to wind-down, chaining breathing into a sleepcast. Off by default at 22:00, on its own Android channel so it can be silenced independently. Calm runs five; one to two captures most of the gain.

Shipped

Extend check-ins beyond mood

Nightly sleep quality, one row per night, notes encrypted on the same path as mood. Gratitude and reflection remain unbuilt and are a smaller loss.

Shipped

Test three tabs against five

Headspace ran this and simplified. Courses and Stats are both reachable from elsewhere; as permanent tabs they may be costing more than they return.

Experiment

Decide on a dated daily original

Calm's freshest pull reason is that today's session did not exist yesterday. This is a content-operations commitment, not an engineering task, and should be decided rather than drifted into.
